Health insurance claim rejected? Here is what to do

In short: A rejection is not the final word. Read the reason in the insurer’s letter, check it against your policy wording, and send a written representation to the insurer’s grievance cell with your documents. If that fails, you can escalate to IRDAI’s Bima Bharosa portal and the Insurance Ombudsman, free of cost.

Why health insurance claims get rejected

The insurer has to give you the reason in writing. These are the reasons we see most often:

  • Pre-existing disease. The insurer says the illness existed before the policy began and its waiting period is not over. Under IRDAI’s 2024 rules, this waiting period cannot be longer than 3 years.
  • Waiting period. Most policies do not pay for illness in the first 30 days (accidents are covered), or for listed conditions such as cataract or hernia for a fixed period. Time already served on an earlier or ported policy counts.
  • Non-disclosure. The insurer says a health condition was not declared in the proposal form. After 5 years of continuous cover, called the moratorium period, a claim cannot be contested on this ground unless there is proven fraud.
  • “Not medically necessary”. The insurer says the treatment could have been given without admission. Your treating doctor’s notes are the main evidence here.
  • Exclusions. The treatment falls under an exclusion listed in the policy, such as cosmetic treatment.
  • Documents or deadlines. Papers were missing, or the insurer was informed late.

Many rejections rest on a narrow reading of one clause. Reading that clause next to your medical records is where most cases are won.

What to do, step by step

  1. Read the rejection letter. Note the clause number and the exact reason. If the reason is vague, ask the insurer or TPA to state it in writing.
  2. Collect your documents. Policy schedule and wording, a copy of your proposal form, the rejection letter, discharge summary, bills, test reports, doctor’s notes and every email or letter exchanged with the insurer.
  3. Check the reason against your policy. Was the waiting period already over? Was the condition really pre-existing, or diagnosed after cover began? Does the exclusion actually apply to this treatment?
  4. Write to the insurer’s grievance cell. Answer the rejection point by point and attach evidence, such as a certificate from your treating doctor. Insurers must acknowledge a complaint within 3 working days and resolve it within 14 days.
  5. Escalate if needed. Register the complaint on Bima Bharosa. If the insurer rejects your complaint or does not reply within a month, you can go to the Insurance Ombudsman within a year. See how the Ombudsman works.

Mistakes that weaken a case

  • Arguing in general terms without quoting the clause the insurer relied on.
  • Sending documents that contradict what was submitted earlier, such as a different date of first diagnosis.
  • Waiting too long. The Ombudsman route closes one year after the insurer’s reply.

Questions people ask

Can a rejected health insurance claim be reopened?

Yes. You can send a written representation to the insurer’s grievance cell explaining why the rejection is wrong, with evidence. Insurers do reverse decisions when the reason does not hold up against the policy wording, though no one can guarantee the outcome.

How long do I have to challenge a rejection?

Act as soon as you can. A complaint to the Insurance Ombudsman must be filed within one year of the insurer’s reply, and a consumer complaint is generally due within two years of the dispute arising.

Do I need a lawyer?

No. The grievance cell, Bima Bharosa and the Insurance Ombudsman are free and do not need a lawyer. We help people who want experienced support through that process.

My cashless request was denied. Is my claim rejected?

Not necessarily. A cashless denial means you pay the hospital and then file a reimbursement claim, which is assessed separately. See our page on health claim reimbursement.
