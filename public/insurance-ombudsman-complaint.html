How to complain to the Insurance Ombudsman

In short: The Insurance Ombudsman is a free forum for disputes with your insurer, including rejected, short-paid and delayed health claims up to ₹50 lakh. Complain to the insurer in writing first. If it rejects your complaint, does not reply within a month, or gives an unsatisfactory reply, you can file with the Ombudsman within one year, online or by post.

Can you go to the Ombudsman?

  • You have first complained to the insurer in writing.
  • The insurer rejected the complaint, did not reply within a month, or you are not satisfied with its reply.
  • You file within one year of the insurer’s reply, or of the month passing without one.
  • The amount in dispute is up to ₹50 lakh.
  • The same dispute is not already before a court, consumer commission or arbitrator.

Which office to approach

There are 17 Insurance Ombudsman offices across India. You can file with the office that covers the insurer’s branch you dealt with, or the one that covers where you live.

How to file, step by step

  1. Keep your complaint to the insurer ready, with its reply or proof that a month has passed.
  2. Fill the complaint form. It is available on the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen website, cioins.co.in, where you can also file online.
  3. Attach your documents: the policy, claim papers, the rejection or settlement letter, your complaint to the insurer and its reply.
  4. State the relief you want, with the exact amount, and keep the acknowledgement.

What happens next

  • The Ombudsman first tries to settle the dispute by mediation.
  • If that fails, it hears both sides, often online, and passes an award within three months of receiving everything it needs.
  • The award binds the insurer, which must comply within 30 days. It does not bind you: if you do not accept it, you can still go to a consumer commission or court.

Questions people ask

Is there a fee to complain to the Insurance Ombudsman?

No. Filing a complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman is free, and you do not need a lawyer.

What is the time limit to approach the Ombudsman?

One year from the insurer’s reply to your complaint, or from the end of the month after you complained if the insurer did not reply.

What is the maximum amount the Ombudsman can award?

The Ombudsman handles disputes up to ₹50 lakh.

Can I go to the Ombudsman if my case is already in consumer court?

No. The Ombudsman will not hear a dispute that is already before a court, consumer commission or arbitrator.
