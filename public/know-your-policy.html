Know your health policy before you need it

In short: Most claim disputes start with something in the policy the family never read: a room-rent limit, a co-payment, a waiting period or a sub-limit. Check these terms in your policy schedule and wording now, while nothing is at stake, and ask the insurer in writing about anything unclear.

The terms to check

  • Sum insured. The most the insurer pays in a year, and whether it is restored after a claim.
  • Room-rent limit. The room you can take, such as a single private room or a percentage of the sum insured. Going above it can reduce the whole claim proportionately.
  • Co-payment. The share of each claim you pay yourself.
  • Sub-limits. Fixed maximums for treatments such as cataract surgery.
  • Waiting periods. Usually 30 days for any illness, a fixed period for listed conditions, and up to 3 years for pre-existing diseases.
  • Exclusions. Treatments the policy never pays for.
  • Pre- and post-hospitalisation. How many days of expenses before admission and after discharge are covered.
  • Claim deadlines. How soon you must inform the insurer and submit a claim.
  • Network hospitals. Where you can get cashless treatment.

Your proposal form matters

The health details in your proposal form are the first thing an insurer checks when a claim comes in. Keep a copy, and make sure every condition and medicine was declared correctly.

After 5 years of continuous cover, called the moratorium period, a claim cannot be contested for non-disclosure unless there is proven fraud.

Renewing and porting

  • Renew on time. A break in cover can restart waiting periods.
  • Porting. When you move to another insurer, the waiting period you have already served carries over, up to your earlier sum insured.

Questions people ask

Where do I find the room-rent limit?

In your policy schedule or the policy wording, usually in the section on hospitalisation expenses. It may be a type of room or a percentage of the sum insured per day.

What is the waiting period for pre-existing diseases?

It depends on your policy, but under IRDAI’s 2024 rules it cannot be longer than 3 years.

What is the moratorium period?

After 5 years of continuous cover, an insurer cannot contest a claim on the ground of non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except in cases of proven fraud.

Do I lose my waiting period if I change insurers?

No. When you port your policy, the waiting period you have already served carries over to the new insurer, up to your earlier sum insured.
